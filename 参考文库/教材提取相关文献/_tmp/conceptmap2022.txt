Abstract
Acquiring knowledge about a topic is a complex process that requires an individual
to gradually understand all concepts related to this topic. The learning experience for in-
dividuals can be enhanced by visualizing the key characteristics that serve as a guide for
learning. Equipping learners with a concept map that orders concepts, represented as no-
des, to be studied according to their prerequisite order, indicated by direct edges, helps
themstayontrack.However,existingalgorithmsforautomaticprerequisitedetectionare
tooinaccurate,whichreduceslearners’trustinsuchmapsasoneassumestheprerequisite
relation to be completely reliable. In this work we propose to replace prerequisite rela-
tions with less authoritative coverage relations, as they indicate only that one concept is
broaderand related toanotherone. Sincemostofthe prerequisitesofagiven conceptare
less difficult than the concept itself, we argue that combining the coverage relation with
concept’s difficulty scores encodes similar semantics as the prerequisite relation. Howe-
ver,duetothecoveragerelationbeinglessauthoritative,potentiallyinaccuratelydetected
coverage relations may be ignored by learners.Such relations are considered more like
recommendations instead of facts. In turn, this change in perception about the reliability
oftheedgesintheresultingconceptmapcreateslessfrustrationforlearners.Further,two
additional aspects, the unstructured nature and the abundance of the learning materials
shouldalsobeconsideredfordevisingasalablemethodforconceptmap’sconstruction.
With this in mind, this thesis aims to automatically construct from unstructured tex-
tual learning materials a concept map that encodes concept difficulty as node color and
